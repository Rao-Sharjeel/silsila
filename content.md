## 2 — Four Orders
*Section id: `#path`*

**Label:** The path
**Heading (Urdu):** چار سلسلے
**Heading (English):** Four inheritances, one chain

**Lead paragraph:**
> Most orders trace authority through a single line. Sarkar Mubarak held khilafat in more than one, and the training he left draws on four. A Saifi disciple inherits all of them at once.

**Four cards** (Arabic name / English name / description):

1. **النَّقْشَبَنْدِيَّة** — Naqshbandiyya
   > The primary root, through its Mujaddidi branch from Ahmad Sirhindi. Silent remembrance, sobriety, and attention held in the breath.

2. **القَادِرِيَّة** — Qadiriyya
   > From Abdul Qadir Gilani. Sarkar Mubarak received khilafat in this line through Sheikh Haji Pachero.

3. **الچِشْتِيَّة** — Chishtiyya
   > From Moinuddin Chishti. Love taken as method, and remembrance that is heard as well as held.

4. **السُّهْرَوَرْدِيَّة** — Suhrawardiyya
   > From Shihab al-Din Suhrawardi. Service, discipline, and the ordered rule of the khanqah.

**Closing line:**
> The four do not compete for the disciple. They are gathered, and handed on together.

## 3 — The Five Subtle Centres (Lataif)
*Section id: `#lataif`*

**Label:** The work

**Qur'anic epigraph** `[locked]`:
- Arabic: اَللّٰهُ نُوْرُ السَّمٰوَاتِ وَالْاَرْضِ
- English: Allah is the Light of the heavens and the earth. The likeness of His Light is a niche, and within it a lamp; the lamp in glass, the glass as though it were a shining star.
- Source: Surah an-Nur 24:35

**Heading (Urdu):** لطائف
**Heading (English):** The five subtle centres

**Lead paragraph:**
> Naqshbandi masters describe faculties within the person, each with its own seat and its own light. Under a shaykh's direction the disciple's attention rests on one, then moves to the next, in order. Followers of Sarkar Mubarak knew him for this work above all.

**The five centres** (interactive — click each dot to read its entry). Default-selected on page load: **Qalb**.

| Centre | Arabic | Seat | Colour | Attributed to | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qalb — the heart | قَلْب | two fingers below the left breast | yellow | Adam | Where the work begins. Remembrance is placed here first and held until it continues without the disciple's effort. |
| Ruh — the spirit | رُوح | two fingers below the right breast | red | Noah and Abraham | Opened once the heart is settled. The attention lifts off the self and turns toward what gave it life. |
| Sirr — the secret | سِرّ | two fingers above the left breast | white | Moses | Secret not because it is withheld from others, but because nothing besides Allah is admitted into it. |
| Khafi — the hidden | خَفِيّ | two fingers above the right breast | black | Jesus | The dimmest of the five and the least described. It is known by the shaykh's report of it, not by the disciple's account. |
| Akhfa — the most hidden | أَخْفَى | the centre of the chest | green | Muhammad ﷺ | The last of the five. Past it the work turns to the nafs, then to the whole body, until nothing is left outside the remembrance. |

**Closing note:**
> The five belong to the *ʿalam al-amr*, the world of command. Beyond them the disciplines continue through the nafs and the four elements to *sultan al-adhkar* — remembrance in the whole body, no part of it left outside.

**⚠ Advisory callout ("Read before practising"):**
> Seats and colours follow the Mujaddidi scheme, and branches differ on the order of the last two. Nothing set down here replaces a shaykh's instruction, and the lataif are not taken up without one.

## 4 — Mamulat-e-Saifia (Daily Practice)
*Section id: `#practice`. Split across two bands — night (before Fajr) and paper (after Fajr) — with a labelled "Fajr" divider between them.*

**Label:** Daily
**Heading (Urdu):** معمولاتِ سیفیہ
**Heading (English):** Mamulat-e-Saifia

**Lead paragraph:**
> What a Saifi disciple is asked to keep, from the last third of the night through the rest of the day.

**Before Fajr:**

1. **Muraqaba** (مراقبہ) — *Last third of the night*
   > Fifteen to thirty minutes of silent sitting after Tahajjud. The nafs is brought under inspection and measured against what has been commanded — not an emptying of the mind, but an accounting.

2. **Qalbi zikr** (ذکرِ قلبی) — *Silent, in the heart*
   > Remembrance placed in the heart rather than on the tongue. Nobody beside you hears it. This is the Naqshbandi mark, and the reason a room of Saifi salikeen at zikr can be almost quiet.

**— Fajr — the light arrives — (فجر)**

**After Fajr:**

3. **Rabita** (رابطہ) — *With the shaykh*
   > An inner connection kept with the shaykh, living or departed, through which guidance is understood to pass. Held quietly through the day's ordinary work.

4. **Silsila and Munajat** (سلسلہ و مناجات) — *Every day, without missing*
   > The chain of masters recited from the present back to the Prophet ﷺ, and supplication after it. The disciple says the names daily so as to stand in a line rather than alone.

5. **Majlis-e-zikr** (مجلسِ ذکر) — *At the khanqah*
   > Gathered remembrance, held together. Followers describe states of wajd in these sittings under Sarkar Mubarak. Times are set by each centre.

## 5 — The Founder
*Section id: `#founder`*

**Label:** The founder
**Heading (Urdu):** اخوندزادہ پیر سیف الرحمٰن مبارک
**Heading (English):** Sarkar Mubarak
**Dates:** 20 Muharram 1344 · 10 August 1925 — 14 Rajab 1431 · 27 June 2010

**Biography, paragraph 1:**
> He was born in Baba Kalai, a village some twenty kilometres from Jalalabad, and had the Qur'an from his father. In the early 1940s he went to Peshawar for tafseer, hadith, usul al-fiqh, aqeeda and tajweed. He was around thirty when he gave bay'ah to Shah Rasul Thaqalayni and was set to silent remembrance.

**Biography, paragraph 2:**
> Maulana Muhammad Hashim Samangani completed that training and granted him autonomous khilafat. He taught in Archi and left Afghanistan before the war reached it. In Bara he built a masjid, a Dar-ul-Ulum and a khanqah — and that is counted as the founding of the Saifia. He held to one condition throughout: the Sunnah first, and no spiritual practice standing apart from it.

**Register of places** (year · place · what happened):

| Year | Place | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1344 AH · 1925 | Baba Kalai | Born near Jalalabad. First instruction, and the Qur'an, from his father. |
| Early 1940s | Peshawar | Formal study in tafseer, hadith, usul al-fiqh, aqeeda and tajweed. |
| c. 1955 | Bay'ah | Allegiance to Shah Rasul Thaqalayni, and initiation into silent dhikr. |
| 1387 AH · 1967 | Khilafat | Granted autonomously by Muhammad Hashim Samangani. Separately, khilafat in the Qadri line through Sheikh Haji Pachero. |
| Before 1978 | Archi | A mosque and Dars-e-Nizami in Afghanistan — from which the name Peer-e-Archi. |
| 1978 | Pir Sabaq | Left Afghanistan ahead of the war. Three years of guidance here. |
| 1409 AH · 1989 | Bara | Masjid, Dar-ul-Ulum and khanqah — the founding of the Silsila-e-Saifia. |
| 1431 AH · 2010 | Ravi Rehan Shareef | Died 14 Rajab. The order's khanqah stands here, outside Lahore. |

**Honorific names** (name — what it means / where it comes from):

- **Sarkar Mubarak** — The blessed master. The name most used among salikeen.
- **Peer-e-Archi** — For the district in Afghanistan where he first taught.
- **Shah-e-Khurasan** — King of Khurasan — the old name for the country he came from.
- **Hazrat Sahib** — How he was addressed inside the khanqah.

**Colophon heading:** Colophon — how this page is set

**Four colophon notes:**

- **Colour** — The five accents are the traditional colours of the lataif and appear nowhere but the lataif. Gold is kept for the chain and for light. The ground runs from a warm night to parchment, turning at Fajr.
- **Type** — Urdu leads and English follows, sharing a left edge. Arabic invocation is always naskh; Urdu content is always nastaliq.
- **Noor** — Every band is lit from its own top edge, the way light falls through the oculus of a dome. In the hero the light of each inherited order runs down its own strand and gathers at the knot, one after another.
- **Structure** — Only one thing on this page is drawn as a sequence, because only one thing is one: the golden chain. The founder's life is set as a register of places, and everything else stands on its own.
