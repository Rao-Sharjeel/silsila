## 1. Overview

**Silsila-e-Saifia** (Urdu/Arabic: سلسلہ سیفیہ) is a Sufi spiritual order (tariqa) rooted primarily in the **Naqshbandi Mujaddidi** tradition, while also drawing on and integrating elements of three other major orders: **Chishtia, Qadria, and Suharwardia**. This multi-order integration is a defining feature the Silsila emphasizes about itself — disciples receive training that synthesizes practices from all four lineages under one chain of authority.

- **Founder:** Akhundzada Peer Saif-ur-Rahman Mubarak (also styled "Sarkar Mubarak," "Peer-e-Archi," "Shah-e-Khurasan," "Kajurai Baba")
- **Founded:** Circa 1409–1410 AH (roughly the early 1990s CE) in Bara, near Nowshera, in what was then Pakistan's North-West Frontier Province (now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa)
- **Main present-day khanqah:** Ravi Rehan Shareef (also spelled Ravi Rayan/Reyan Shareef), Lakhoder, on the outskirts of Lahore, Pakistan
- **School of law (Madhhab):** Hanafi
- **Creed (Aqidah):** Maturidi
- **Denomination:** Sunni Islam, aligned with the Barelvi devotional tradition (the founder cited Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi among his influences)
- **Estimated reach:** Followers ("Saifi Salikeen") described in the order's own literature as numbering in the hundreds of thousands to over a million, spread across Pakistan, Afghanistan, the UK, Europe, the US, Canada, Fiji, India, Bangladesh, Norway, and parts of the Middle East

## 2. The Founder: Akhundzada Saif-ur-Rahman Mubarak

### Life dates
- **Born:** 10 August 1925 CE (20 Muharram 1344 AH), in the village of Baba Kalai, roughly 20 km from Jalalabad, Afghanistan
- **Died:** 27 June 2010 CE (14 Rajab 1431 AH)

### Early life and education
- Received his first religious instruction — including Qur'an study — from his father, described in sources as Sufi Hafiz Qari Muhammad Sarfraz Khan.
- In the early 1940s he moved to Peshawar for formal religious education, where he studied **tafseer** (Qur'anic exegesis), **hadith**, **usul al-fiqh** (principles of jurisprudence), **aqeeda** (creed), and **tajweed** (Qur'anic recitation).

### Spiritual training and lineage
- At around age 30–32, he took **bay'ah** (spiritual allegiance/initiation) with the Naqshbandi Sheikh **Shah Rasul Thaqalayni** (also rendered "Shah Rasool Taloqani"), receiving initiation into silent dhikr practice.
- He was later directed to **Maulana Muhammad Hashim Samangani**, who renewed and deepened his spiritual training and eventually granted him an autonomous **khilafat** (spiritual deputyship/successorship), reportedly in 1387 AH.
- He separately received **khilafat in the Qadri order** through **Sheikh Haji Pachero**.
- The order's own literature frames his lineage as also drawing spiritual inspiration/authority from **Abu Hanifa**, **Baha-ud-Din Naqshband Bukhari** (founder of the Naqshbandi order), **Abdul-Qadir Gilani** (founder of the Qadri order), **Moinuddin Chishti** (founder of the Chishti order), **Ahmad Sirhindi** (Mujaddid Alf-e-Thani, founder of the Mujaddidi reform branch), and **Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi**.

### Founding the order and later life
- Established a **masjid, Dar-ul-Ulum (seminary), and khanqah** in Bara (Afridi tribal area near Nowshera) for spiritual training — this is treated as the founding of the Saifia order/branch, c. 1409–1410 AH.
- Prior to this he had also built a mosque and ran Dars-e-Nizami (traditional seminary curriculum) courses in the Archi district of Afghanistan — hence the honorific "Peer-e-Archi."
- Faced persecution under Afghan authorities; sources note his **arrest on 27 April 1987**. He was released and fled Afghanistan, settling for a period in **Pir Sabaq**, a town in what is now Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan, where he continued his Naqshbandi spiritual guidance.
- He had reportedly emigrated from Afghanistan around 1978, ahead of the Soviet-Afghan War.
- In Bara, he later faced opposition and hostility from Mufti Munir Shakir (a Deobandi cleric) and Lashkar-e-Islam activists, including anti-Saifi propaganda via local FM radio and reported destruction of property.
- The order's khanqah network eventually centered on **Ravi Rehan Shareef / Lakhoder** on the outskirts of Lahore, which functions as the movement's main present-day spiritual center.

### Teachings and reputation
- Emphasis on **strict compliance with the Sunnah** as the foundation for any spiritual practice — i.e., Shariah and Tariqa are treated as inseparable, with orthodox practice as the non-negotiable base layer beneath any mystical/spiritual training.
- Known in follower literature for **"activating the lataif"** (subtle spiritual centers/faculties within the disciple) and for gatherings of dhikr in which participants reportedly experienced **wajd** (spiritual ecstasy).
- Credited by followers with facilitating conversions to Islam among attendees at his gatherings.
- Honorific titles used for him include **Sarkar Mubarak** ("Blessed Master"), **Peer-e-Archi**, **Hazrat Sahib**, **Kajurai Baba**, and **Shah-e-Khurasan**.

## 3. Doctrine and Identity

- **Shariah–Tariqa unity:** The order's teaching materials are explicit that the spiritual path (tariqa) is built on top of — never a substitute for — orthodox Islamic law and practice (Shariah). This is presented as a corrective to any perception of Sufism as antinomian or separate from mainstream Sunni observance.
- **Multi-silsila integration:** Unlike orders that trace authority through a single chain, Saifia formally integrates **four** transmission lines — Naqshbandi, Qadri, Chishti, and Suharwardi — under its founder's combined khilafat. This is treated as a distinguishing credential.
- **The "Golden Chain" (Silsila):** As with all Sufi orders, legitimacy rests on an unbroken master-to-disciple chain of transmission tracing back to the Prophet Muhammad. Daily recitation of this chain (paired with Munajat/supplication) is part of the order's prescribed practice — see below.
- **Barelvi alignment:** The founder's citation of Ahmad Raza Khan Barelvi as an influence situates the order within the Barelvi devotional current of South Asian Sunni Islam (veneration of the Prophet, saints, and shrine culture), as distinct from Deobandi reformism — a distinction that appears to have generated real friction historically (see the Bara conflict with Mufti Munir Shakir, above).

## 4. Practices — "Mamulat-e-Saifia" (Daily Disciplines)

The order prescribes a structured set of daily spiritual exercises for disciples:

| Practice | Description |
|---|---|
| **Muraqaba** (meditation) | Silent introspective meditation, typically 15–30 minutes, often performed after Tahajjud (pre-dawn voluntary prayer) or Fajr (dawn prayer). Understood as inspection/purification of the *nafs* (lower self) in light of divine commands. |
| **Qalbi Zikr** (heart-remembrance) | Silent remembrance of Allah centered in the heart rather than vocalized — consistent with the broader Naqshbandi emphasis on silent dhikr (*dhikr khafi*) as opposed to the vocal dhikr more associated with Chishti practice. |
| **Rabita** (spiritual connection with the Shaykh) | Maintaining an inner connection/focus on the living or deceased Shaykh as a means of spiritual transmission and guidance — a standard but sometimes theologically debated Naqshbandi practice found across many branches of the order. |
| **Daily Silsila recitation + Munajat** | Reciting the chain of spiritual masters ("golden chain") back to the Prophet, paired with supplication (munajat), as a daily act of remembrance and alignment with the lineage. |
| **Congregational dhikr gatherings** | Group dhikr sessions (sometimes producing *wajd*/ecstatic states in participants, per follower accounts) held at khanqahs — daily or scheduled, per local center practice. |

## 5. Geography and Institutions

- **Main khanqah:** Ravi Rehan Shareef (Ravi Rayan/Reyan Shareef), Lakhoder, on the outskirts of Lahore, Punjab, Pakistan. Referred to in some materials as "Astana Aliya Muhammadia Saifi Ravi Rayan Sharif."
- **Earlier centers (historical, tied to the founder's life):**
  - Archi district, Afghanistan — mosque and Dars-e-Nizami courses
  - Nowshehra, Pakistan — early spiritual training center
  - Pir Sabaq, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa — three years of post-exile religious guidance
  - Bara, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (Afridi tribal area) — site of the mosque, Dar-ul-Ulum, and khanqah considered the order's founding institution
- **Global presence:** Followers/branches reported in the UK, continental Europe, the US, Canada, Fiji, Afghanistan, India, Bangladesh, Norway, and parts of the Middle East — though specific branch khanqah addresses outside Pakistan were not found in this research pass and would need direct confirmation.

## 6. Leadership and Succession — ⚠️ Needs Verification

Public web sources are **thin and inconsistent** on this point — treat everything below as a lead to confirm, not a fact to publish.

- The name that recurs across secondary sources (Wikipedia's article on the founder, an "islaminperspective.com" pamphlet, social-media pages tied to Ravi Rayan Shareef) is **Peer Mian Muhammad Hanfi Saifi** (also rendered "Hazrat Peer Saif Ur Rehman Mian Muhammad Hanfi Saifi"), associated with the "Astaana-e-Alia" at Ravi and referred to in follower social media (TikTok, YouTube, Facebook groups) as a current spiritual teacher/leader connected to annual **Urs** (death-anniversary commemoration) gatherings for the founder.
- No source found in this pass gives a clear, formal, citable account of:
  - The exact date/process of succession after the founder's 2010 death
  - Whether leadership is singular (one Sajjada Nashin/successor) or distributed among multiple khalifas at different centers
  - The full current title and formal role of Peer Mian Muhammad Hanfi Saifi within the order's structure
- **Recommendation:** Before publishing any "Current Leadership" page, get this confirmed directly from the khanqah/community — Sufi succession claims are frequently contested between rival claimants, and getting this wrong on a public site could be read as taking a side in an internal dispute.
