Origins

A Gathering of Rivers

سلسلہ سیفیہ



Silsila-e-Saifia

For centuries, the great Sufi orders of Islam — Naqshbandiyya, Qadiriyya, Chishtiyya, and Suhrawardiyya — developed along distinct paths, each carrying its own methods of remembrance, discipline, and spiritual training. Silsila-e-Saifia is where these four rivers meet.

Founded by Hazrat Peer-e-Archi Akhundzada Saif-ur-Rahman Mubarak (R.A.), the order was established not to replace these paths but to unite them — offering a single chain of transmission through which a seeker may draw on the full breadth of Sufi tradition, grounded firmly in the Hanafi school of jurisprudence.

النقشبندية

Naqshbandiyya

The Naqshbandi order traces its silent, heart-centered method of remembrance back through an unbroken chain to Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (R.A.), the first Caliph of Islam. Known for its emphasis on inward remembrance over outward expression, discipline of the self, and closeness to the Prophetic Sunnah, it remains one of the most widely practiced Sufi paths across Central and South Asia.

الچشتية

Chishtiyya

Brought to South Asia by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti (R.A.), the Chishti order is renowned for its emphasis on love, tolerance, and openness — expressed through practices such as sama (spiritual audition) and a deep concern for serving humanity regardless of faith or background. It remains one of the most influential Sufi orders on the subcontinent.

القادرية

Qadiriyya

Founded by Shaikh Abdul Qadir Jilani (R.A.) in 12th-century Baghdad, the Qadiri order is among the oldest and most widespread Sufi paths in the Muslim world. It is distinguished by its balance of spiritual devotion with adherence to Islamic law, and by a tradition of scholarship, humility, and service that spread the order from the Middle East to nearly every corner of the Islamic world.

السهروردية

Suhrawardiyya

Established by Shaikh Shihabuddin Suhrawardi (R.A.), the Suhrawardi order is marked by its rigorous integration of Islamic jurisprudence with spiritual training, producing scholars and saints who bridged the outward sciences of Islamic law with the inward sciences of the heart. Its influence spread through Persia, Central Asia, and the Indian subcontinent.

The remembrance

اذکارِ اربعہ

The Zikar of the four Silsilas

A Saifi disciple does not inherit one order's remembrance but four. Select a silsila to see how its zikr is kept — drawn from Mamulat-e-Saifia, as taught to enrolled disciples.

Seven centres rather than five — the two of ʿalam al-khalq, the self and the body, taken up after the five of ʿalam al-amr are settled.

قلب

Qalb — the heart

  • Seattwo fingers below the left breast
  • Colouryellow
  • Attributed toAdam

Where the work begins. Remembrance is placed here first and held until it continues without effort.

The Naqshbandi zikr proper: the kalima's negation and affirmation, circulated silently through the body rather than spoken aloud. Breath is held from below the navel; لا is drawn up and out to the shoulders as negation, clearing the heart of everything but Allah; اِلَّا اللّٰہ is then struck onto the qalb lataifa as affirmation — a single continuous circuit, not a straight line.

  • لَا مَعْبُوْدَ اِلَّا اللّٰہThere is no object of worship but Allah.
  • لَا مَقْصُوْدَ اِلَّا اللّٰہThere is no aim but Allah.
  • لَا مَوْجُوْدَ اِلَّا اللّٰہThere is nothing truly existent but Allah.
  • لَا مَطْلُوْبَ اِلَّا اللّٰہThere is nothing sought but Allah.
Before beginning

اللّٰهُمَّ يَا لَطِيْفَ الْاَحْفٰى اَنْتَ مَقْصُوْدِيْ وَرِضَاكَ مَطْلُوْبِيْ، اَعْطِنِيْ مَحَبَّتَكَ وَمَعْرِفَةَ ذَاتِكَ وَصِفَاتِكَ

Read once, then held silently through the day's rounds. A thousand a day is the measure the mashaikh give.

Thirty-six stations, walked in order under a shaykh's direction — from the five lataif themselves through to a station past every definition. Select one to read its niyyah.

1 · Qalb

Istighfar, first

اَسْتَغْفِرُ اللّٰهَ الَّذِيْ لَآ اِلٰهَ اِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّوْمُ وَاَتُوْبُ اِلَيْهِ

313 times daily, best after Tahajjud before true dawn, or after Asr if missed. It stands outside the nine asbaq proper, but the Qadiri Saifi mashaikh require it regardless.

The nine asbaq

Mirrors the Qadiri shape but opens from the opposite end, with ہُوْ rather than the negation-affirmation formula. Chishti muraqabat aren't fixed by day-count the way Naqshbandi ones are — Sarkar Mubarak had Chishti disciples draw on the Naqshbandi muraqabat instead of a separate sequence.

The nine asbaq

The Suhrawardi asbaq are identical to the Qadiriyya's nine, word for word — the same istighfar, the same nine lessons in the same order. The silsila's own character shows only in its muraqaba, which closes the sequence rather than sitting mid-way through it.

The closing muraqaba Five to twenty minutes, no fixed upper limit. Facing Madina Munawwara, eyes closed: a straight road, on which the prophets, saints and angels of heaven and earth, the shaykhs of the silsila, and everyone present in the gathering are walking together. The intention is to visit, and to present that day's zikr as a gift at the Prophet's ﷺ threshold — forming a circle in his presence, offering the reward of the day's practice, asking leave to withdraw, then withdrawing backward out of respect rather than turning one's back, until home again, and the eyes open.

The counts, seats and sequence above follow Mamulat-e-Saifia as compiled for enrolled disciples. Nothing set down here replaces a shaykh's instruction, and none of this is taken up without one.

The foundations

اہم اصول

Seven principles every disciple keeps

Before the wird, before the muraqaba, these govern whether either bears fruit — the ground the rest of the practice is built on.

01

Adab-e-Shaykh

Respect and proper conduct toward one's Shaykh — trusting his direction, and following it without contest or complaint.

02

Pabandi-e-Salah

Steadfastness in the five daily prayers, kept to their times without excuse.

03

Daily Zikr

The given zikr, done as much as possible, every day — Allah loves the remembrance that is kept up consistently, however small.

04

Rabta

The disciple's inward connection to the Shaykh, held in the heart and renewed daily — the channel through which faidh reaches him.

05

Gunahon se Bachna

Guarding against sin — the heart cannot hold both light and heedlessness at once.

06

Tawakul

Trust that Allah alone is enough — placing one's affairs in His hands after taking the lawful means.

07

Walidain ka Adab

Respect and good conduct toward one's parents — a duty the Qur'an places beside the worship of Allah Himself.

Daily

معمولاتِ سیفیہ

Mamulat-e-Saifia

What a Saifi disciple is asked to keep, from the last third of the night through the rest of the day.

  • Last third of the night

    مراقبہ

    Muraqaba

    Fifteen to thirty minutes of silent sitting after Tahajjud. The nafs is brought under inspection and measured against what has been commanded — not an emptying of the mind, but an accounting.

  • Silent, in the heart

    ذکرِ قلبی

    Qalbi zikr

    Remembrance placed in the heart rather than on the tongue. Nobody beside you hears it. This is the Naqshbandi mark, and the reason a room of Saifi salikeen at zikr can be almost quiet.

فجر

Fajr — the light arrives

  • With the shaykh

    رابطہ

    Rabita

    An inner connection kept with the shaykh, living or departed, through which guidance is understood to pass. Held quietly through the day's ordinary work.

  • Every day, without missing

    سلسلہ و مناجات

    Silsila and Munajat

    The chain of masters recited from the present back to the Prophet ﷺ, and supplication after it. The disciple says the names daily so as to stand in a line rather than alone.

  • At the khanqah

    مجلسِ ذکر

    Majlis-e-zikr

    Gathered remembrance, held together. Followers describe states of wajd in these sittings under Sarkar Mubarak. Times are set by each centre.

The founder

اخوندزادہ پیر سیف الرحمٰن مبارک

Sarkar Mubarak

20 Muharram 1344 · 10 August 1925  —  14 Rajab 1431 · 27 June 2010

He was born in Baba Kalai, a village some twenty kilometres from Jalalabad, and had the Qur'an from his father. In the early 1940s he went to Peshawar for tafseer, hadith, usul al-fiqh, aqeeda and tajweed. He was around thirty when he gave bay'ah to Shah Rasul Thaqalayni and was set to silent remembrance.

Maulana Muhammad Hashim Samangani completed that training and granted him autonomous khilafat. He taught in Archi and left Afghanistan before the war reached it. In Bara he built a masjid, a Dar-ul-Ulum and a khanqah — and that is counted as the founding of the Saifia. He held to one condition throughout: the Sunnah first, and no spiritual practice standing apart from it.
